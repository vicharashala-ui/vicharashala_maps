The Ramsar Convention is an international treaty for wetland conservation, and India's list of Ramsar sites has grown substantially in recent years to become one of the largest in Asia. A large share of these wetlands exist *because* of a nearby river — they're floodplain lakes, oxbow remnants, or delta systems that a river created and continues to sustain.

## Floodplain and oxbow wetlands

Many Ramsar sites in the Gangetic plain are old river channels. As a river like the [Ganga](/river/ganga) or [Gandak](/river/gandak) shifts course over decades, it leaves behind crescent-shaped oxbow lakes that become permanent wetlands. Nawabganj Bird Sanctuary and several of Uttar Pradesh's wetland reserves formed this way — they're not fed by a river channel today, but their basin shape is a direct fingerprint of one.

## Delta wetlands

At the coast, river deltas form some of India's most significant Ramsar sites. Chilika Lake, at the mouth of several small rivers feeding the [Mahanadi](/river/mahanadi) delta system in Odisha, is Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon and a critical wintering ground for migratory birds, including flamingos arriving from as far as the Iranian and Central Asian wetlands. The [Godavari](/river/godavari) delta similarly supports Ramsar-recognized mangrove wetlands where fresh river water meets the sea.

## High-altitude and glacial-fed wetlands

Not every river-linked Ramsar site is in a plain or delta. Chandertal, in Himachal Pradesh, is a high-altitude lake fed by glacial meltwater that eventually reaches the [Chenab](/river/chenab) system — a reminder that "river-linked wetland" covers a wide range of elevations and climates, not just floodplains.

## Why the count keeps growing

India has been adding Ramsar sites steadily over the past several years as more state-level wetlands complete the designation process, and river-fed wetlands make up a large share of new additions — partly because they're well-documented (states already track them for flood management and irrigation) and partly because they tend to have the bird and fish diversity Ramsar criteria look for.
