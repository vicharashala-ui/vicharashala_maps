Tiger reserves come up constantly in UPSC prelims (matching reserve to state) and mains (conservation policy, human-wildlife conflict, corridor connectivity), so it's worth understanding the network as a system rather than memorizing a list.

## How the network is structured

India's tiger reserves operate under Project Tiger (launched 1973, now overseen by the National Tiger Conservation Authority) and each reserve is legally divided into a core zone — where human activity is heavily restricted — and a buffer zone, which allows regulated use and acts as a transition to the surrounding landscape. This core-buffer structure is itself a common prelims question: know that the distinction is about permitted land use, not habitat quality.

## Rivers as reserve boundaries and corridors

Many tiger reserves are built around a river valley, and the river often does double duty as a water source for wildlife and a natural corridor connecting reserves. Corbett Tiger Reserve, India's oldest national park (established 1936, later the first Project Tiger reserve), sits along the [Ramganga](/) river system in Uttarakhand's Terai region. Bandhavgarh and Kanha in Madhya Pradesh anchor the central Indian tiger landscape, which links westward toward the reserves along the [Narmada](/river/narmada) valley. In the south, [Bandipur](/) and Nagarhole, both in the [Kaveri](/river/kaveri) basin, form part of a contiguous forest block with Mudumalai in Tamil Nadu and Wayanad in Kerala — together one of the largest tiger populations in the world.

## Corridors matter more than any single reserve

A recurring exam theme: tiger conservation depends on connectivity between reserves, not just protection within reserve boundaries. Isolated reserves fragment gene pools and push tigers into conflict with people at the edges. Rivers frequently form the physical corridor that keeps two reserves ecologically connected — which is one more reason river geography and protected-area geography are worth studying together rather than as separate topics.

## Studying the map

Because tiger reserves cluster so heavily along river valleys, it's often faster to learn them by basin than by state. [Vicharashala Maps](/) lets you toggle the tiger reserve layer alongside any river's course, and each river's detail page lists the protected areas along it — a useful way to build the state-river-reserve associations prelims tends to test.
